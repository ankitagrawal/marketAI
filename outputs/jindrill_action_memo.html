01 Why this one

Of the 28 companies screened, Jindal Drilling is the only one showing the full positive pattern at once — revenue up, margins up, cash flow up, debt down — sustained across five years rather than one lucky quarter. It's also priced like nobody has noticed: 9.6× trailing earnings and 3.5× EV/EBITDA, versus a business whose operating margin nearly tripled.

That combination is rare enough in this basket to be the one name worth acting on. Everything else in the screen was either financially strong with a fatal flaw (Sandesh's core business shrinking, Dalmia's returns stuck near 7%) or thinly documented enough to just be a guess.

03 The one thing that has to check out

36–61% of reported profit in most years has come from "other income" — treasury and interest income sitting on the balance sheet, not day-rate revenue from drilling. In the December 2025 quarter, other income swung negative and the company posted an outright loss despite the operating business apparently being intact.

That's not disqualifying — a debt-free driller sitting on cash will always show some of this — but it means the 9.6× P/E is not obviously cheap until you know how much of the ₹216 Cr FY25 profit was rig operations versus interest on the cash pile. No rig count, day-rate, utilization %, or contract-expiry data was available in this pass. That is the actual gap between a screen and a position.

04 What to verify before sizing a position

  1. Pull the Q1–Q2 FY27 concall transcript directly jindaldrilling.com or the BSE filings archive — not aggregator summaries. This wasn't accessible during the screen; it's the single highest-value next step.
  2. Separate operating profit from other income, quarter by quarter Rebuild EBITDA and PAT excluding treasury/interest income for the last 8 quarters. If the margin story survives that cut, the thesis holds.
  3. Get the rig fleet detail Number of rigs, current utilization, day-rates being realized, and contract expiry dates — ideally against ONGC or whichever charterer is the counterparty. Single-customer concentration is common and unquantified here.
  4. Explain the December 2025 loss quarter directly from the filing Confirm it was other-income volatility, not a one-off operating problem, before treating it as noise.

05 Case for and against

Supports the thesis

  • Margin expansion (11%→35%) has held for five years, not one cycle
  • Debt cut by nearly three quarters while growing revenue
  • Negative working-capital days — the business is self-funding
  • No share dilution in the period; EPS growth is genuine
  • Cheapest valuation of any name in the top five of the screen

Works against it

  • Large, unexplained swings in non-operating income drive PAT
  • ROE (~10%) is unremarkable despite the margin story
  • Zero visibility into rig contracts, the actual demand driver
  • Offshore drilling is a capex-cycle business — day-rates can reverse
  • Thin analyst coverage; concall access was the failure point twice

06 What would kill this thesis

If the concall confirms operating EBITDA margin is materially below the 35% headline once treasury income is stripped out — or if rig utilization is concentrated in one contract expiring within 12 months — this drops out of consideration entirely. A repeat of a negative-other-income quarter without a clear explanation would also be a reason to walk away rather than average in.
